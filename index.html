          Partner FAQ: before your first conversion

          Draft client-facing copy written from Alpaca's public guides. It would need review by Product, Legal and Compliance before use.

          What do we need in place before our first mint?
          1. Sign up as an authorized participant with each token issuer you plan to use.
          2. Integrate with Alpaca's Broker API or Trading API, and hold the underlying shares in an account in your name.
          3. Send Alpaca's integration team the email you used for the issuer sign-up and your Alpaca account number. Alpaca then links your two accounts with the issuer.
          4. Register your receiving wallet with the issuer. Some issuers also require the address to be whitelisted.
          5. Confirm the issuer offers your token on the network you want.
          What does “pending” mean?

          Your request was accepted and is moving through the steps between Alpaca and the issuer: checks, moving the underlying shares, delivering tokens and confirming. It becomes completed when the issuer confirms the tokens were delivered, or rejected if a check fails.

          Why was our mint rejected?

          The most common reasons in the public guides: not enough of the underlying stock in your account, an account not yet authorized for tokenization, a wallet not registered to you with the issuer, a token not offered on the requested network, or a quantity with more than 9 decimal places.

          Can we retry a mint request without creating a duplicate?

          For mint requests, Alpaca's guide for authorized participants documents an optional Idempotency-Key header. Repeating the same key with the same request returns the original response instead of creating a second mint. Reusing a key with a different request is refused, and replaying a request that was originally rejected returns an error. Confirm retry behavior for other endpoints with Alpaca before relying on it.

          Why is a tiny token balance left after we redeem?

          Alpaca handles quantities to 9 decimal places, and issuers typically round redemptions down to that precision. Tokens with more decimals, such as 18-decimal tokens on Ethereum, can leave a very small amount unconverted.

          How do we track a request?

          List your requests, or look one up by Alpaca's request ID or by your own client_request_id. Each record shows its status, transaction hash, fees and timestamps.

          Does a token give us shares?

          It depends on the product. Alpaca's site notes that third-party tokenized assets give economic exposure, not direct equity ownership or voting rights, while DTCC tokenized entitlements are described as keeping the rights of the traditional security. Check each issuer's terms.

          How this was built

          The question it answers

          Which partner should we prioritize, what stops it from becoming operational, and how will we know a launch is commercially successful?

          Taken from public sources

          • Prerequisites, mint and redeem steps, request fields and the three request statuses, from Alpaca's tokenization guides for authorized participants and issuers.
          • Rejection reasons, idempotent retries and the 9-decimal precision rule, from the same guides.
          • Network names used in the data appear in the guide's list of supported networks.

          Proposed, not public

          • Stage-level detail inside a pending request, owners, time targets (4 hours to flag, 24 hours to escalate) and the definition of repeat activity.
          • All partners, requests, volumes and the fee. Issuers are fictional (Issuer Alpha, Issuer Beta).
          • Event trails on requests are simulated evidence. The public API exposes a request's status, transaction hash, fees and timestamps, not each internal step.
          • The weekly brief is a read-only scenario: KPIs are calculated from the sample records, while decisions, risks and actions were written for this scenario.

          What the public API does provide

          • Request fields: tokenization_request_id, type (mint or redeem), status, underlying and token symbols, quantity, issuer, network, wallet, tx_hash, fees, created_at, updated_at.
          • Statuses: pending, completed, rejected.
